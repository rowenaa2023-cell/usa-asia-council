Sen. Tim Kaine and Korean War Divided Families

Date & Time February 06, 2025
2:00 PM – 4:00 PM EST
Location / Venue Russell Senate Office Building, Room 325, Washington, DC
Featured Speakers Senator Tim Kaine, Korean American Divided Families National Coalition Representatives

Executive Overview

Senator Tim Kaine joined aging Korean-American family members and humanitarian advocates in a poignant congressional briefing advocating for urgent humanitarian reunions between Korean Americans and their family members stranded in North Korea. With the armistice signed over seven decades ago, surviving family members are now in their eighties and nineties, making congressional action intensely time-critical.

Key Deliberations & Strategic Briefing

Testimonies from elderly family members recounted the painful separation from siblings and parents since 1953. Senator Kaine reiterated his bipartisan legislative push directing the Department of State to coordinate with South Korean authorities and the Red Cross to prioritize virtual and in-person family reunions on purely humanitarian grounds, unencumbered by stalled denuclearization talks.

The briefing culminated in a signed letter from over 40 members of Congress urging the Special Envoy for North Korean Human Rights Issues to expedite formal dialogue channels.

Strategic Policy Takeaways

  • Congressional momentum for the Korean American Divided Families National Registry Act.
  • Bipartisan consensus treating elderly family reunions as an urgent humanitarian emergency.
  • Coordination protocol between U.S. State Department consular officials and Seoul’s Ministry of Unification.
  • Launch of an oral history digital archive capturing testimonies of first-generation survivors.
"Time is not on our side. Providing these elderly families the opportunity to hold their loved ones before they pass is a moral imperative that transcends politics." — Senator Tim Kaine
