Trump's Saudi Nuclear Deal Guts Nonproliferation Safeguards and Risks a Middle East Arms Race

Recent developments surrounding a proposed multi-billion dollar nuclear cooperation agreement between the U.S. and Saudi Arabia have raised severe alarms across the global nonproliferation community. By bypassing the stringent 'gold standard' safeguards of Section 123 of the Atomic Energy Act, this deal sets a dangerous precedent.

Foreign policy experts warn that transferring sensitive nuclear technology without strict guarantees against uranium enrichment and spent-fuel reprocessing risks igniting a Middle East arms race. If Saudi Arabia develops dual-use capabilities, it will inevitably provoke regional rivals, including Iran, to accelerate their own nuclear ambitions. The ripple effects of such instability would be disastrous not only for the Middle East but for broader U.S. security interests globally. We advocate for immediate congressional oversight to mandate robust nonproliferation guardrails before any technological transfer occurs.
