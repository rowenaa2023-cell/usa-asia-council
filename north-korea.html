Our Missions · Northeast Asia Security

Managing the North Korean Nuclear Threat

North Korea's relentless advances in intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) technology, tactical nuclear warheads, and military cooperation with Moscow pose a direct threat to the American homeland and our key allies in Seoul and Tokyo. Unilateral summits without substantive working-level preparations have repeatedly failed to dismantle Pyongyang's nuclear complex.

A viable strategy requires ironclad extended deterrence, reinforced by the historic U.S.-Japan-South Korea trilateral security framework established at Camp David. Real-time missile warning data sharing, joint naval exercises, and integrated regional missile defense systems ensure that any military provocation by the Kim regime will be met with overwhelming, unified resistance.

Simultaneously, the United States must maintain realistic diplomatic channels to pursue step-by-step threat reduction, enforce maritime interdiction against illicit oil smuggling and cryptocurrency theft, and shine an unyielding spotlight on the regime's egregious human rights abuses against the North Korean people.
